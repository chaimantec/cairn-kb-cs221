# CS221 — Artificial Intelligence: Principles and Techniques | Autumn 2025 — Percy Liang

Stanford's introductory AI course. Covers perception, reasoning, action, and learning under resource constraints, drawing on symbolic, neural, and statistical AI traditions. This knowledge base covers lectures 1–8 of 20, with edited transcripts, slide transcriptions, and interlinked wiki pages.

## Lectures

- **[Lecture 1 — Course Overview and AI Foundations](wiki/01-course-overview.md)** — What is AI? The four ingredients of intelligence (perceive, reason, act, learn), resource constraints, alignment and societal goals, history of AI through three traditions (symbolic, neural, statistical), and an introduction to tensors and einops.
- **[Lecture 2 — Learning I](wiki/02-learning-i.md)** — Einops review, gradients and partial derivatives, computation graphs and backpropagation (building a mini-PyTorch), and linear regression as the first complete ML pipeline: hypothesis class → loss function → optimization algorithm.
- **[Lecture 3 — Learning II](wiki/03-learning-ii.md)** — Linear classification: from zero-one loss (zero gradients → optimization fails) through logistic regression (maximum likelihood with a smooth, differentiable loss), logistic function and its properties, multiclass extension via softmax and cross entropy, and representing text as tensors (tokenization → one-hot → bag of words).
- **[Lecture 4 — Learning III](wiki/04-learning-iii.md)** — Deep neural networks: PyTorch as NumPy + autodiff, the feature-map insight (linear in φ-space = nonlinear in x-space), MLPs with ReLU activation (why two linear layers don't help but one ReLU does), deep networks and the vanishing/exploding gradient problem, and five survival techniques: residual connections, layer normalization, Xavier initialization, activation function choice, and SGD/Adam optimizers.
- **[Lecture 5 — Search I](wiki/05-search-i.md)** — Search as reasoning: the search-problem abstraction (start_state, successors, is_end), the travel problem and what belongs in the state, future cost and exhaustive search (exponential time), dynamic programming = exhaustive search + caching, approximate search via best-of-n and beam search, and test-time compute in language models cast as a search problem (the Bitter Lesson's "search and learning").
- **[Lecture 6 — Search II](wiki/06-search-ii.md)** — Search with cycles: uniform cost search (past costs in increasing order, priority queue, correctness proof) and A* (UCS with heuristic-modified costs, consistency and admissibility, telescoping-sums correctness). Then the relaxation recipe for heuristics: knock down walls (Manhattan distance), free tram (fewer states), overlapping tiles (independent subproblems), and combining heuristics with max.
- **[Lecture 7 — Markov Decision Processes](wiki/07-markov-decision-processes.md)** — Search with stochastic actions: the MDP formalism (states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, discount), policies as solutions, rollouts and expected utility, Monte Carlo estimation, and the two exact algorithms — policy evaluation (value of a given policy) and value iteration (value and construction of the optimal policy), differing by a single max. Running examples: the flaky tram (walk until 5, then tram) and the dice game (stay: 12 > 10).
- **[Lecture 8 — Reinforcement Learning](wiki/08-reinforcement-learning.md)** — MDPs without the model: the agent/environment loop, get_action and incorporate_feedback, and four algorithms on the flaky tram — model-based value iteration (estimate the MDP, then value-iterate: explore −9, exploit −6.6), model-free Monte Carlo (average rollout utilities into Q-values), SARSA (bootstrap mid-rollout, on-policy), and Q-learning (bootstrap off-policy to estimate Q*). The leaderboard and the epsilon-greedy exploration/exploitation balance.

## Topic Pages

- **[Tensors](wiki/tensors.md)** — Multi-dimensional arrays, the atoms of modern ML. Tensor order, shapes in different domains (vision, language, tabular), creating and viewing tensors, matrix multiplication and broadcasting, and why tensor ops are efficient.
- **[Einops](wiki/einops.md)** — Named-axis tensor operations for readable, self-documenting code. `einsum` for generalized matrix multiplication, `reduce` for axis aggregation, and `rearrange` for splitting/merging dimensions (essential for multi-head attention).
- **[Backpropagation](wiki/backpropagation.md)** — Reverse-mode automatic differentiation. Computation graphs, the chain rule, forward and backward passes, node-level gradient rules (Add, Multiply, Squared), and how PyTorch works under the hood.
- **[Linear Regression](wiki/linear-regression.md)** — The simplest complete ML pipeline. Hypothesis class (parameterized linear functions), squared loss, and training loss minimization via gradient descent. Demonstrates the general ML template.
- **[Gradient Descent](wiki/gradient-descent.md)** — Iterative optimization by moving in the negative gradient direction. Learning rate tradeoffs, convex vs. non-convex convergence, and variants (SGD, Adam).
- **[Search Problems](wiki/search-problems.md)** — The abstraction that turns word problems into code: state, successors (action–cost–next-state), is_end, and the minimum-total-cost objective. What belongs in the state, why keeping it small matters, and the Bitter Lesson motivation.
- **[Dynamic Programming](wiki/dynamic-programming.md)** — Exhaustive search plus caching: Bellman's terminology, the future-cost recurrence, the three-line diff from exhaustive search, and when the cache pays off (states fit in memory, many paths to a state).
- **[Beam Search and Best-of-n](wiki/beam-search.md)** — The two approximate search methods: n random rollouts of a policy, and keeping the best `beam_width` partial solutions. The width-1/infinity extremes, determinism vs. stochasticity, the policy-as-prior distinction, and particle filtering.
- **[Uniform Cost Search](wiki/uniform-cost-search.md)** — Dijkstra's algorithm for cyclic search problems: past cost vs. future cost, the explored/frontier/unexplored buckets, priority-queue mechanics (in-place updates, stale entries), the correctness proof, and why non-negative costs are required.
- **[A* Search](wiki/a-star-search.md)** — UCS with heuristic-shifted costs: ordering by PastCost + h(s), the modified-cost trick, consistency (and admissibility), correctness via telescoping sums, the efficiency bound, and the counterexample of a bad heuristic.
- **[Heuristics from Relaxation](wiki/heuristics.md)** — How to construct heuristics that are consistent by construction: solve a relaxed problem (fewer costs) and use its future cost. The three structured relaxations (closed form, fewer states, independent subproblems), fair accounting, and max-combining heuristics.
- **[Markov Decision Processes](wiki/markov-decision-processes.md)** — Search problems with stochastic actions: the formal definition (T, R, γ), why the solution must be a policy, rollouts and discounted utility, and the two running examples (flaky tram, dice game).
- **[Monte Carlo Policy Evaluation](wiki/monte-carlo-evaluation.md)** — Estimating V_π by averaging rollout utilities: the 1/√n convergence rate, the coin-flip intuition, and the lecture's numbers (−11.7 vs the true −12).
- **[Policy Evaluation](wiki/policy-evaluation.md)** — Computing V_π exactly: Q-values as the recurrence's building block, bootstrapping, the L∞ convergence test, the two phases of convergence, and the tram/dice demos (27 iterations → −12; 2/3-decay → 12).
- **[Value Iteration](wiki/value-iteration.md)** — Computing V* and the optimal policy: the max-over-actions recurrence (one line from policy evaluation), Bellman's 1957 foundation, the flaky-tram optimum (walk until 5, then tram; −7.33), and why the optimal policy is deterministic for MDPs.
- **[Reinforcement Learning](wiki/reinforcement-learning.md)** — MDPs where the agent doesn't know T or R: the agent interface (get_action / incorporate_feedback), policy vs. agent, the four-algorithm map (model-based vs. model-free, MC vs. bootstrapping, on- vs. off-policy), epsilon-greedy exploration, and the world-models debate.
- **[Q-Learning](wiki/q-learning.md)** — Estimating Q-values without the MDP, in three steps: Monte Carlo averaging (wait for the episode), SARSA (bootstrap mid-rollout, on-policy), and Q-learning (bootstrap off-policy to target Q*). The gradient update, the one-line code difference, and policy improvement.
